Arrangements include horizontal/vertical crossing, five diagonal edge crossings, four fully outside edges and an inside control. A120x120 translated plain render provides an unclamped geometry/cropped-pixel control. Sampling assertions use observed plain bounds for fuzz0; positive-fuzz fixtures check support/RNG agreement and tile24-or-larger acceptance rather than asserting an independently calculated window width.

128 textured fixtures draw and52 reject. Every tile at least24x24 draws, even for endpoints beyond both sides of the viewport. At fuzz0, horizontal and vertical crossing windows shrink from65x3 or3x65 in the large control to24x3 or3x24. Tile24 therefore succeeds even though the unclamped geometry exceeds it. Tile23 rejects those cases. This is corner clamping before the size guard, not wrapping oversized textures.

2,184 exact fuzz0 sample assertions confirm that cropping is centered around the clamped raster window; destination x/y samples that window directly. Accepted textured/plain pixel support, RNG and tables agree. Rejected cases preserve initial RNG/tables. Positive fuzz reserves horizontal margins during corner clamping, so the final window remains within the viewport width. This conclusion is supported by inspected draw order and the accepted tile24 controls, not a full independent corner-arithmetic trace.

Plain corner-clamped output differs from a crop of the large translated render in nine of twelve cases: all five diagonal crossings and four fully outside arrangements. The horizontal, vertical and inside controls match. Differences range from20 pixels for outside-edge collapse to100–105 for diagonal crossings. A fully outside line can collapse onto a viewport border and still draw20 pixels; it is not trivially culled. These synthetic controls show why a smaller viewport can alter geometry and texture eligibility. They do not establish native clipping semantics or the full composed camera behavior.
